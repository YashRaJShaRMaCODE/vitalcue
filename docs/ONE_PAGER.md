# AgeWell (VitalCue) — synthetic health-model learning workshop

**A practical, instructor-led session on how to question a health-model output—using fictional data only.**

## The problem

Data-science educators need concrete examples to teach model inputs, outputs, limitations, privacy, and responsible interpretation. Generic notebooks and course catalogs provide useful building blocks, but an instructor may still need to assemble the lesson, setup steps, safety framing, and discussion materials. This problem and willingness to pay are hypotheses; validate them with instructors before selling.

## The offer

A bounded workshop for a university data-science, AI-literacy, or privacy class, delivered by an instructor/facilitator. The proposed pilot includes:

- A prepared fictional-data demo and setup checklist.
- A guided exercise tracing inputs, a binary label, and the limits of interpretation.
- A learner worksheet, instructor run-of-show, and discussion rubric.
- A debrief on model provenance, evaluation, privacy, and failure modes.

Pilot length, class size, delivery mode, fee, and success measure are set with the buyer after discovery. No public price, customer, result, or revenue is claimed yet.

## Why this approach

VitalCue's potential distinction is the focused safety lesson: learners inspect what a small model does and explicitly learn what it cannot establish. It complements established learning platforms and notebook environments; it does not replace them or claim superior model quality. The workshop can be adapted to an instructor's objectives after a short discovery call.

## Safety and trust

**Education only. Not a medical device or healthcare service.** No clinical validation is claimed. Use the supplied fictional examples only; do not enter real/identifiable health information. Outputs must not guide care. Do not collect health values, upload records, send predictions by email, or use them in analytics. Distribution or paid use depends on verified model/data/package rights and a working, smoke-tested demo or approved local fallback.

## Pilot proof to collect

With instructor permission, record the learning objective, whether setup worked, instructor-preparedness rating, learner-reported clarity of the limitations, time/cost to deliver, and the buyer's decision to repeat or pay. Do not collect learner health information. Do not publish quotes, logos, or outcomes without explicit permission.

## Next step

Interview 5–8 instructors about their current lesson, the setup work they do, the learning outcome they need, budget/approval path, and what would make a pilot worth paying for. Revise or stop if the pain or buyer is not supported.
